Hotel Booking Cancellations
A Business Analysis

Every cancelled hotel booking is a room the hotel held, a guest it planned for and revenue it expected but never received. In this project I take the role of a business analyst supporting the management of two hotels in Portugal, a city hotel in Lisbon and a resort in the Algarve. The business problem was clear: too many bookings were being cancelled. Management wanted to know where the cancellations were coming from, how much they were costing, and which policy change would protect the most revenue. Using 119,390 real bookings from July 2015 to August 2017, I defined the stakeholders, business questions and KPIs, cleaned the data in SQLite, answered ten business questions with simple SQL, and built a Power BI dashboard for management.

The analysis showed that 37.5% of the 117,429 valid bookings were cancelled, putting up to €16.7 million of revenue at risk against €26.0 million earned. The City Hotel was hit hardest, cancelling 42.2% of its bookings compared with 28.1% at the resort. The clearest driver was how far ahead guests booked: only 9.7% of bookings made within a week were cancelled, but 57.2% of those made more than six months ahead never happened. Online travel agents lost the most money, €10.2 million, while group bookings had the highest cancellation rate at 61.7%. The most surprising result came from the deposit policy, where non-refundable bookings were cancelled 99.4% of the time. Instead of reporting that figure at face value, I investigated it: 97% of these bookings came from Portuguese groups and travel agents, booked 213 days ahead on average, which suggests rooms blocked in advance and released later. Because the hotel may have kept those deposits, the real loss is likely nearer €13.1 million, a point I recommend confirming with Reservations and Finance before any policy is changed.

Business Analysis in SQL

The analysis was done in SQLite using DBeaver. The first step builds a clean bookings table, calculates the number of nights and the value of each booking, and removes 1,961 bookings with no nights or an impossible price. Each business question then uses simple SQL: COUNT and SUM for the KPIs, CASE to group bookings by how far ahead they were made, and GROUP BY to compare hotels, channels, deposit types and months.

Every query has a comment explaining why the question matters to the business and what the result was. A follow-up query investigates the surprising deposit result before drawing any conclusion.

Power BI Dashboard

The dashboard gives management the whole picture on one page. Four cards show total bookings, cancellation rate, revenue earned and revenue lost, and a hotel filter lets each manager switch between the city and resort hotels. Every chart title states its finding, so the page can be read in seconds.

The charts show the cancellation rate rising steadily with lead time, the unusual non-refundable deposit result, online travel agents losing the most revenue, and summer months, led by August, losing the most money.

The Business Problem

Cancellations hurt a hotel twice: the revenue it planned on disappears, and the room cannot be sold to someone else in time. Before touching the data, I wrote a project brief setting out the objective, the stakeholders and what each of them needs. The General Manager needs the size of the problem and a clear recommendation. The Revenue Manager needs to know which channels, booking windows and months lose the most. Sales needs to know which partners cancel most, Front Office needs the busiest months for staffing, and Marketing needs to know whether loyal and engaged guests are more reliable.

These needs became ten business questions and four KPIs: cancellation rate, revenue earned, revenue lost and lead time (the number of days between booking and arrival).

The Business Case

The first recommendation targets 39,178 bookings that were made more than 90 days ahead with no deposit. Of these, 36.5% were cancelled, losing €7.0 million over the 26 months of data.

If a deposit prevented, or paid for, just a quarter of those cancellations, the hotels would protect about €1.76 million, roughly €0.8 million a year. Because the policy only affects bookings made far in advance, guests who book closer to their stay, who already cancel least, are not affected.

Next steps would be to confirm the non-refundable figures with Finance, agree the deposit level with the Revenue Manager, and track the cancellation rate monthly on the dashboard after the change.

Key Findings

  1. The earlier the booking, the riskier it is. Cancellations rise from 9.7% within a week to 57.2% for bookings made over six months ahead.
  2. The City Hotel has the bigger problem. It cancels 42.2% of bookings and loses €10.9 million, almost twice the resort.
  3. Online travel agents cost the most. They account for €10.2 million of lost revenue. Groups cancel most often, at 61.7%.
  4. The deposit data needs checking. Non-refundable bookings cancel 99.4% of the time, almost all from groups and agents in Portugal.
  5. Engaged guests are reliable guests. Repeat guests (16.2%) and guests who make special requests (22.0%) cancel far less than others.

Recommendations

  1. Ask for a deposit on early bookings. Require a deposit for bookings made more than 90 days ahead.
  2. Tighten group and agent contracts. Release unsold blocked rooms earlier and limit how many rooms can be held without payment.
  3. Investigate before acting. Confirm with Reservations and Finance how non-refundable bookings are recorded and whether deposits were paid.
  4. Engage guests before arrival. Send pre-arrival emails inviting special requests, and reward repeat guests with a loyalty offer.
